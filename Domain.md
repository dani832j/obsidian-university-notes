### Definition

The domain of a function is the set of all inputs for which

the function is defined.

We often denote the domain by
$$
D.
$$
___
### Example

Let

$$
f(x)=\sqrt{x}.
$$

Since square roots of negative numbers are not real,

$$
x \geq 0.
$$

Therefore the domain is

$$
D=[0,\infty).
$$
---
Let

$$
f(x,y)=\frac{1}{x+y}.
$$

The denominator cannot be zero.

Therefore

$$
x+y \neq 0.
$$

So the domain is

$$
D=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2 : x+y\neq0\}.
$$

### Notes

The domain consists of all allowed inputs.

Before working with a function, it is often useful to determine its domain first.

Restrictions usually arise from:

- Division by zero
- Square roots
- Logarithms
